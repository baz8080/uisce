## PR #26 — Add a midday build, for publication latency only
*merged 2026-07-31 · +40/−3*

### PR body

Adds a second scheduled build at ~12:45 UTC. The justification is reader-facing freshness and nothing else — the comment in `build.yml` now says that explicitly, because the old one didn't.

## Why a second build, and why midday specifically

Notices publish between 07:00 and 16:00 UTC (staffed office hours, already documented in `notes/data-quality.md`). That means a second build only helps if it lands *inside* that window — the instinctive "add one 12 hours later" is nearly worthless.

Simulated against the actual publication-time distribution (8,135 cases, May–Jul):

| schedule (UTC) | mean latency | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1x 18:45 (current) | 7.7h | 7.5h | 11.1h |
| 2x 06:45 / 18:45 | 6.8h | 7.4h | 10.4h |
| **2x 12:45 / 18:45** | **3.9h** | **3.4h** | 5.6h |
| 3x 06:45 / 12:45 / 18:45 | 3.5h | 3.3h | 5.5h |

The third build buys 0.4h, so this stops at two. Gain is uniform across the classes that matter — burst mains 8.7h → 4.0h, boil-notice-flagged 7.0h → 3.3h.

Cron is set to `20 11` rather than `20 12` because scheduled runs land ~1h20 after the cron fires (observed across the last ten runs: cron `23 17`, runs landing 18:14–18:55).

## Why this is *not* a data-quality change

The previous comment framed the build gap as the resolution of `cases.closed_at` and noted that daily "cannot remove" the never-observed-open floor. That reads as an open invitation to keep shrinking the gap. It's now closed off, because the floor past daily isn't ours:

- The feed marks a case `Closed` a median of **75.7h** after the works actually finished (p25 +57.2h, p90 +85.2h; **97% land more than 24h late**), comparing observed transitions against the inferred completion for the 484 cases with both. The build gap is a small quantisation on top of a much larger administrative lag.
- The never-observed-open rate is already **12% → 1.9%** since the cron went daily (18 of 933 cases across ten daily builds), six of them `investigation`, which is maintenance severity and feeds no published number.
- Those 18 aren't fast events narrowly missed — they're notices posted at or after completion, the negative-span family already documented in `data-quality.md`. No cadence catches those open.

## Reviewer notes

**The concurrency group is unrelated to the schedule change.** 12:45 and 18:45 are 6h apart against 1–3 minute runs, so the schedule cannot collide with itself. It guards a pre-existing hazard the run history shows happening: a manual dispatch landing on a scheduled run. Both would download the same release DB, and the later publish silently discards the earlier run's observations — permanent, because `closed_at` is a one-shot observation the feed can never replay. `cancel-in-progress: false` is deliberate: a cancelled run has already read the feed and would lose the same way. Happy to split this into its own PR if you'd rather keep the schedule change narrow.

**`data-quality.md` had to change, not just gain a note.** The existing paragraph asserted a single cadence-change date and pointed at "shrink the gap further" as the remedy, so leaving it as-is would have made it wrong. The new subsection also sizes the second discontinuity in the closure series: at most 11 of the 18 were even *published* before the new midday slot (a generous upper bound — it assumes each was still `Open` at that moment), so the undercount moves 1.9% → ~1.1% at best. Against a resolved panel carrying a median of 53 events per county-month, that's under half an event, and `resolvedSection` renders one month at a time with no delta or trend, so there's no rendered comparison for it to corrupt.

**Rollout:** GitHub reads `schedule:` from the default branch only, so the new cron does nothing until this merges.

**Worth a follow-up check:** the ~1h20 offset is fitted to ten runs at a single time of day. Midday scheduler contention may differ — worth reading `first_seen` after a few days and nudging the cron if it drifts off 12:45.

## PR #27 — Rank the largest disruptions, and stop charging recurring windows as continuous outages
*merged 2026-08-02 · +11113/−58*

### PR body

Two related pieces of work. The first adds a page; the second fixes what that page made impossible to ignore.

## The national top ten (`#top`)

Person-hours were computed per county and per area, never per disruption, so "what actually happened in July" returned 26 county rows rather than the burst that caused them. The ten largest events are **21.9% of July's national person-hours**, one of them 9% alone, so the distribution earns a page.

Complete months only — the in-progress month reshuffles between builds as open events accrue toward the 14-day cap. Person-hours are clipped to the month so the ranking sums against the county figures already published.

The end badge counts **notices, not events**. `Region.observed_end` is OR'd across an event's pins, and July's largest has 18 pins of which exactly one reported a completion; badging that "completion confirmed" would present a plan as a measurement. The payload carries `pins` / `confirmed` / `scheduled` instead.

Counties are deliberately not ranked: by raw person-hours that is a population ranking, and per-capita it is arithmetically the availability column already on the overview.

## Classification leak found while building it

A title no `CATEGORY_RULE` claims gets `work_category = NULL`, and NULL sat in `REPAIR_CATS` — so every spelling the table missed was classified as a hard supply outage and accrued person-hours. 66 cases were unmatched; the largest group was 19 restriction notices spelled `Water Conservation/Restriction` against the rule's plural, and one of them ranked **#9 nationally** while being, correctly categorised, a degraded event that accrues nothing.

Almost every unmatched title was a near-miss on an existing slug. Three new slugs were needed, and one exposed a sharper bug: a `Lifting of Do Not Consume Notice` was stored as `consumption_notice_issued` — the opposite claim, and one that knocks a grade off Cork. It was a stale value from an earlier rule set, surviving because `backfill_work_category` only ever sets a slug and never clears one.

NULL no longer defaults to an outage, and every backfill now prints the title prefixes it could not place. That guard found the Cork lift on its first run.

## Recurring windows

A notice reading *"daily from 10pm until 7am, from 9 July to 27 July"* is eighteen nights of nine hours. It was charged as **385.2 continuous hours**, which made one Donegal event 9.9% of July's national person-hours and the top row of the new page.

The extraction was never at fault — the v2 prompt already recognised recurring windows and reported the last date at the closing time correctly. The loss was in the *representation*: one end instant cannot express a repeating schedule. Prompt v3 reports the window itself and `resolve_case` expands it, so a `Case` now carries a list of intervals. `merge`, `union_seconds`, `region_month`, `top_events` and the day bars already handled lists, so `Region.add` was the only change.

`local_date` and `local_time` keep their pv2 meaning exactly, which let `uisce-eval-replay` validate v3 with no new labelling: **identical to pv2 on both rounds** — 99/114 on round 1, 120/120 on the unseen uniform round, zero parse errors across 234 rows. Round 1 also turned out to hold the only labelled recurring cases: 8 rows, all of which pv1 got wrong and pv3 holds.

## Results

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| National July person-hours | 27,505,846 | **25,395,359** (−7.7%) |
| Donegal July | 3,700,792 / 97.023% | **2,118,941 / 98.295%** |
| Donegal per-capita | 22,156 h/1k | **12,682 h/1k** |
| July's largest event | 2,540,854 @ 385.2h | **949,824 @ 144.0h** |

The per-capita row is the point. The methodology notes promise readers that letter grades are assumption-dependent but *county ordering is real*, and Donegal sat at double the field on the strength of one notice's missing field. It is now 12,682 against Clare's 9,588. Donegal's day bar still shows all 31 July days — the fix reduces the price, not the visibility.

## Two things that went wrong, and what they taught

**The first v3 run barely moved anything** (−0.4%, against a −5.9% projection). A completion-update pin reports no window — reasonably, since a finished job has no forward schedule — and because coverage is unioned per `reference_num`, that one pin's continuous interval re-covered every gap its seventeen siblings had carved out. So a window is now treated as a property of the *works*: `event_windows` lends the window any pin reported to pins that reported none, still clipped to the borrowing pin's own start and end.

**The report built to catch exactly that failed to fire.** It grouped only pins that had *claimed* a window, so a pin claiming nothing — the pin doing the damage, tagged indistinguishably from a burst main — never entered the check. Found by verifying the flagship's numbers rather than trusting a clean-looking report. Fixed, with a test for that pin.

## Guards

No eval round labels the window fields, so three standing checks stand in: the close-time cross-check (validated against 8 labelled rows), `unquotable_windows` (every window value should be quotable from the description the model read — 89 of 97 exact, 0 flagged), and the per-build recurrence report. All print on every run, matching `backfill_work_category`'s discipline.

## Not done

- **Recall is unmeasured** — a window the model missed entirely. The pool is 56 notices, of which ~9 are outage-class; that is the labelled round worth doing, and it needs harness work (`FIELDNAMES`, a quota key, its own `ROUND_GLOB`).
- **`area_of` is still first-pin-wins** while the top ten uses dominant-over-union, so one event can be labelled differently in two places. 4.7% of events, changes no published number.
- 29 cases initially failed on context length; LM Studio raised to 8,192 and they re-ran clean. The prompt grew 6,255 → 9,746 chars, which is worth checking against the context before the next corpus run.

`origin/top-ten-page` is an ancestor of this branch and can be deleted once this merges.

## PR #28 — Name an event once, over its whole footprint
*merged 2026-08-02 · +121/−16*

### PR body

Follow-up to #27, which is now merged. One commit, no published figure moves.

## The problem

The open list and the national top ten named the same event by different rules:

- `area_of` took the **first pin the feed published**
- `top_events` took the area holding the **largest share of the footprint**

So a multi-pin burst could read "Allenwood" in the county drill-down and "Prosperous" in the national ranking — same page, same event. **348 events (4.7%) disagreed**; of multi-pin events, 37.6%.

## The change

`build_site` unions each event's footprint as it goes and names it once after the loop; `top_events` reads that name rather than deriving its own. 14 open-case labels change. **The top ten does not move** — it was already using the better rule, so the open list has come to it rather than the other way round.

National July stays at 25,395,359.

## The subtlety

`TownLookup.dominant` gains an `allowed` filter. Shares are summed per area, so a secondary area reached by several pins but the home of none can out-total every pin's own winner:

| | Exton | Wyeville | Zedbury |
|---|---|---|---|
| pin 1 sees | — | 60 | **90** |
| pin 2 sees | **100** | 60 | — |
| union | 100 | **120** | 90 |

Wyeville wins the union while being no pin's home, so it would never have been registered in `county_towns` — and the page renders such a code as a blank `—` heading and silently drops it from the area table's open counts. No event in the corpus does this today; restricting the answer to the pins' own homes makes it unrepresentable rather than unlikely. The test fixture is built to produce exactly that shape, and checks all three answers differ.

## Found but not fixed

Four open cases are filed under an area with no breakdown entry — **identically before and after this change**, so it is neither caused nor cured here. They are advance notices dated far enough ahead that their token footprint lands in no listed month, so their area never earns an entry. The front end already degrades to `—`.

Worth stating because my first draft of the last test asserted "every open case resolves in the county breakdown", which those four falsify. The test now states the narrower invariant this change actually buys: naming cannot invent an area that no pin of the event chose.

It keeps its place — the day bars and event counts read those intervals, and `end_recurrence` is exactly what this rule keys on, so the v3 extraction is what makes the rule possible — but nothing multiplies by a window's length any more. ## The labels: 2 correct, 9 wrong

I had predicted that count and **misread the cause**. I framed the eight "charged as outage" rows as an extraction bug — model failed to emit the window, therefore prompt v4 and another eleven-hour corpus run. The review notes said the classification is wrong on its own terms, which needs neither.

Rule (A) only needs to know *whether* a notice announces a repeating window. **Detection is the easy half**; the window *values* are what needed a language model.

## The fix

The two signals are complementary and fail in opposite directions, so `recurring_events` uses both:

| signal | catches | misses |
|---|---|---|
| notice text | every completion notice the extraction suppressed — 33 events, including all 8 labelled | the enumerated form |
| model extraction | *"from 10am until 6pm on 5 May, 6 May and 7 May"*, which names its days rather than saying "daily" — 5 events | completion notices |

The extraction suppresses the window whenever a completion phrase is present: it is told a completion takes priority over a scheduled end, and applies that to the window fields too. That is a v3 prompt bug, and this routes around it without a re-run.
