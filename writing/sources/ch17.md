## PR #62 — Bring the county-page link up out of the footer
*merged 2026-08-26 · +148/−12*

### PR body

The link to `c/{county}.html` was a paragraph in the footer, shown only in the county view. Nothing gets clicked in a footer, and what is on the other side — the full notice history, the month table, the area list — is more interesting than that placement implied.

### The link moved, and the row href changed with it

It now sits on its own line directly under the county heading, above the month tabs: the placement lifts has always used and esb adopted alongside this.

**The overview row's `href` was the bigger hole.** It pointed at the `#county/…` fragment. lifts and esb both point theirs at the static page with the click suppressed, so a crawler, a middle-click and a "copy link address" all reach the page while a normal click stays in the app. Here they reached nothing durable, which left `c/{county}.html` discoverable only from the footer link and from `areas.html`. Verified in-browser: the href resolves to the page and a click still lands on `#county/Carlow`.

### The label says "every month", not "every notice"

It first shipped as "Every notice ever recorded in Co. Carlow", carried over from the footer copy without being checked against the page. That was false: `_county_events_html` caps at `COUNTY_EVENTS_SHOWN = 60` and prints its own "N older notices not shown here", so any county past 60 had a link the page then contradicted.

What the page really carries that the view does not is *every month* — which is word for word the relation esb's county page stands in to esb's county view, so the two sites now say the same sentence. That leaves two categories rather than three: esb and uisce name the months; lifts names the address, because its page is the same content as its view and there is no difference to name.

### The meta description had the same shape

"…in Co. Carlow - 1,234 notices across 56 areas" — the counts are the county's whole record while the page stops at 60. Reordered so the counts are stated as the county's and the page's contents named after them, which also means a truncated snippet stays true rather than degrading into an inventory claim. `{len(areas):,} areas` printed "1 areas"; fixed in passing, since it is read in a search result.

### The area view is an accepted gap, not an oversight

An area is reachable only as the `#area/…` route and has no page of its own, so a reader drilling county → area watches the affordance disappear — one click into the ~1,836 areas that are the long tail, where a shareable URL is worth most.

Left open deliberately. Closing it means building area pages, and the measurement that gates that decision is written down: of 3,717 areas, **909 are named CSO settlements and 2,808 are electoral divisions whose names all begin "Around "** — pages for those would be thin content at scale. Slugging the county-plus-name pair is collision-free across all 3,717 (name alone collides 185 times), so the scheme is not the obstacle; deciding which areas deserve a page is. A test asserts the area view offers nothing, so the gap stays deliberate.

### Verification

450 tests pass, ruff clean. `tests/test_permalink_affordance.py` is new and covers the placement, the row href, the label's accuracy (it may claim the months, it may not claim every notice) and the area gap; `tests/test_site.py` gains guards for the description's ordering, truncation property, length and plural.

Rendered light/dark/375px with no horizontal overflow. Note the screenshots I worked from were built from the test fixtures — the ArcGIS feed is unreachable from the sandbox — so the layout is real but those numbers are synthetic.

## PR #63 — Give every named area a page
*merged 2026-08-26 · +574/−63*

### PR body

An area was reachable only as the `#area/…` route — not a URL a reader can keep or a search engine can index — and areas are this site's long tail: 1,960 have had a notice, against 26 counties. 739 now have a page at `a/{county}/{area}.html`, and the app's area view links to it. **The indexable surface goes from 28 URLs to 767.**

Closes the gap `notes/frontend-notes.md` recorded as accepted on 2026-08-26.

### Which areas get one — the whole decision

Of the 1,960 areas with a notice:

| kind | with a notice | page? |
|---|---|---|
| CSO settlement | 697 | ✅ |
| City Local Electoral Area | 42 | ✅ |
| Electoral Division | 1,193 | ❌ |
| City residual (`-rest`) | 5 | ❌ |
| Unplaced | 23 | ❌ |

The EDs are why there is a predicate at all. Every one of the 2,808 in the CSO file is named *Around …* — countryside around somewhere, not a place anyone types into a search box — and 1,193 near-identical pages is what a search engine demotes as scaled thin content, with the damage landing on the county pages that already work. The `-rest` codes are a city's leftover LEAs, named *Elsewhere in Cork city*. Neither is an address.

**No notice-count floor on top, deliberately.** A floor of two would drop 122 pages, but it would make a URL appear the day an area's second notice arrives — and a permalink that comes and goes is worse than a short one. A page reading "one notice, this date, four hours, about 500 people" is a real answer about a real place. Median 5 notices per page, mean 9.0, max 83 (Dún Laoghaire).

### The slug ships in the payload, and that is load-bearing

I had planned to derive it in the app. statusui's own test says the two slug functions are *deliberately unpaired*, and `ui.js`'s leaves a fada as a dash: `Dún Laoghaire` → `d-n-laoghaire` where the file is `dun-laoghaire`. 17 names carry a fada and 3 more carry punctuation the two treat differently — **deriving the href client-side would 404 on 20 places.**

So `towns[code].slug` is shipped, present exactly when the area has a page, which makes it the flag as well as the value.

The path is keyed on county-and-name, not the code: a code is not a filename (31 contain a slash, most contain colons — the reason the history shards are per county), and a name alone is not unique because 185 repeat across counties. The pair is unique over all 3,717 areas, asserted rather than assumed.

### The label names the address, not the content

"Permanent link to Abbeydorney" — lifts' wording. The page carries the same notices the view does, uncapped, so naming its content would promise a reader what they are already looking at. That puts this site on both sides of the rule settled earlier: its **county** link names the months, because that page holds months the view does not; its **area** link names the address.

### Two things a review caught (`bba0aa9`)

Both were mine and neither was guarded:

- **The "open the interactive map" link was unroutable.** It shipped as `#area/{county}` — one segment where the app's area route needs two — so it matched neither of the router's patterns and dropped the reader on the national overview. It is the county route now, which is what the link text says and what `county_page_html` already used. The test reads the two patterns out of `site.html` and runs them against the emitted href rather than pinning a remembered shape.
- **A people figure was unqualified.** `people` is the whole event's footprint, and an area page states the area's own Census population two lines above the list — so a notice spanning five areas printed "3,775 people" on a page headed "528 people · Census 2022" with nothing to reconcile them. The app's badge carries that caveat in its title and the port had dropped it. The multi-area note carries it now, and only where there is a figure to qualify.

Both new guards were checked by reintroducing the bug and watching them fail.

### What it costs, and who pays

- **739 standalone pages, ~15.1 MB raw / 4.58 MB gzipped.** Not a bundle — nobody downloads that. A reader landing on one fetches exactly one file: measured at 1 request, ~6.2 KB gzipped (7.8 KB for the largest). No `data.js`, no shard, no external stylesheet.
- **`data.js` grows 849,664 → 865,759, which is +4,517 bytes gzipped (+4.4%)** — the one cost every front-page visitor pays, area pages opened or not.
- 84% of a small page is the inlined CSS. That is the trade `assemble()` makes on purpose, and these are the cold-entry-from-search pages that justify it. Worth re-deciding past ~1,000 pages: a linked stylesheet would drop ~12 MB from the artifact and cost each cold reader a round trip.

## PR #64 — Assert the shared sub-line rule applies, not just that it exists
*merged 2026-08-26 · +104/−20*

### PR body

Tests only. One of three — the same guard lands in esb and lifts, which have the same rule on the same kind of page and the same blind spot.

### The gap

`.chead + .sub` styles the line under a drill-down heading — here, the "Permanent link to …" line on every area page — and it reaches this repo **only through the statusui pin in `uv.lock`**.

When it moved upstream earlier today, the local copy had to be kept back for one commit, because `uv.lock` can only track statusui's `main`. Dropping both at once would have left that line unstyled on the deployed site **with every test still green** — nothing asserted a rule *applied*, only that files said what they said. The failure would have been silent.

### What "applies" decomposes into

Three things, all checkable from a build without a browser:

1. **The page renders an element the rule can match.** A rule with no target does not apply. Checked against the built markup, with a lookahead that keeps the closing tag the `.chead`'s own so a nested div cannot fake the adjacency.
2. **The rule is in the stylesheet that page inlines.**
3. **Nothing in the cascade beats it.** base.css also carries `header .sub`, which sets both properties — it loses on specificity (one class + one type against two classes), and the test resolves that rather than assuming it, in every media context.

Plus a fourth: the rule is upstream and **has not been copied back**, which is the three-identical-copies regression the move existed to end.

### Reuse rather than a second parser

`test_site_css.py` already had a cascade engine for the hidden-vs-display guard — `@media` descent, specificity, source order. `_rules` gains a property argument and `_display` becomes `_winning`; no new CSS parser appears beside the existing one.

`_matches` reads only a selector's subject and assumes the rest, which is why adjacency is checked against the built markup instead of inferred from a match.

### Two of three properties, deliberately

`area.html` takes `margin-bottom` back to 20px on the same selector, and a separate test says so. That override is the mechanism working; asserting the whole shared block survived would have made a correct override look like a regression.

### Verification

Each guard was checked by making the failure it exists for:

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| remove the rule from base.css (the exact regression) | 4 tests fail |
| rename the element the page emits | adjacency test fails |
| copy the rule back into `site.css` | 2 tests fail |

485 tests pass, ruff clean.

A browser-based computed-style test would prove more, but this repo's CI has neither node nor a browser, and by these projects' own standard a check that always skips is worse than none.

## PR #66 — Send a search hit to the area page it names
*merged 2026-08-27 · +165/−17*

### PR body

✅ **baz8080/statusui#8 is merged and `uv.lock` is re-pinned to `c564efa` on `main`.** This is ready to merge on its own now.

Typing "Abbeydorney" and clicking the hit landed you on Co. Kerry, to find Abbeydorney again yourself. The area pages shipped on 2026-08-26 and the one control a reader actually uses could not reach them.

That was the index's shape, not a routing choice: `searchHits` returned `[name, county]` and the matched name was discarded, so `pick: goCounty` was the only thing the callback could do.

## What changed

`search.js` carries the area's slug beside its name, and every hit is a real link — an area to `a/<county>/<slug>.html`, a county to `c/<county>.html` with the click kept in the app.

**The destination is the page, not this site's own `#area` view.** Routing search there — and building esb one so the sites match — was the obvious symmetric answer and is rejected in the note: the two are the same content, the view has no month tabs and no search box in it, `go()`'s own comment already records that pushState does not get a drill-down counted, and the page is indexable, shareable and middle-clickable where a fragment is none of those. It is the argument that moved the overview's county rows off their hash last week, applied to the control that had not had it yet. esb does the same thing with the same code and needs no `#area` route to do it.

**The `#area` view stays** — it is the only surface the 1,221 pageless areas have (delete it and 62% of noticed areas become unreachable), and a drill-down is not an entry point. Its towns rows did carry the same hole the county rows had, so they now point at the page where there is one and the hash where there is not.

## The gate is the payload's slug, not `area_has_page`

The concern the old comment raised is real and is written down rather than deleted: the archive grows without bound, and if a bound is needed again it should be a byte budget, since a count was always a proxy for bytes and a poor one.

Three surfaces named the same destination three ways and one of the three was false: the static footers called areas.html "every area in Ireland", but area_index reads the built history, so an area that has never had a notice is not in it. Everything now says "every area with a notice", which is the directory's own heading. The app is "Co. X's interactive view" everywhere rather than "the interactive map" - it is bars and a table. Counts read "· N notices" on both the static pages and the app cards. ul.notices and ul.areas take back the user agent's 40px padding-inline-start: base.css resets margin and not padding, so list-style: none removed the marker and left the gutter it sat in. Kept per-site rather than promoted, and guarded against the assembled cascade rather than the source text.

.method was stacking margin and summary padding on top of the footer chrome base.css already provides; both are scoped to .card > .method now, the one context that has none of its own. #topLink lost its 14px bottom margin, which was adding to the footer's own 28px.

The county card's hours tile carried a duration and a breakdown of cases in one caption. The split decomposes the outage count, so it rides on the tile that carries that count. completed_n stays in the payload - it is what median_completion_h is computed over.
