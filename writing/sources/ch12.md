## PR #40 — Charge unclosed cases a typical span in availability, not zero
*merged 2026-08-15 · +635/−139*

### PR body

## The problem

Not every case Uisce Éireann opens is properly closed. Those events were kept out of the published median **and** given a token 1-second footprint in the availability arithmetic. The first is right; the second was not.

Availability divides person-disruption-seconds by a denominator fixed by population and calendar, so an event that supplies no duration supplies a **zero** — "exclude" and "impute 0" are the same operation, and there is no third option the way there is for a median. One second is not a conservative reading of a burst main; it is a claim that it disrupted nobody.

## The population is not what its name suggests

Of 4,473 outage-class events, 204 took the token footprint:

| reason | events |
|---|---|
| end extracted but **precedes publication** → span nulled in `build.py` | 200 |
| genuinely `not_found` — no end signal anywhere | 4 |
| still open with no signal (true right-censoring, separate branch) | 29 |

So this is overwhelmingly "closed, with a known end, and a broken start" — the feed re-stamps `STARTDATE` in place ([measured 2026-07-20](notes/data-quality.md)). Those 200 anchor **backwards** from the end they do know, so the hours land on the days the works ran rather than the day the notice finally went up.

## Why the median still excludes them

1. **The project already drew this line.** 894 `scheduled_end_with_time` events are excluded from the headline because a plan is not an observation. An imputed category median is *weaker* evidence than a published plan, so letting imputations in while keeping plans out would be incoherent.
2. **Complete-case analysis is valid here.** Only the outcome variable (duration) is incomplete, and two checks say missingness is unrelated to it: Kaplan-Meier with the open-no-signal events censored gives **13.9h against the naive 13.4h**; calibrating `closed_at` against observed completions implies **~10.7h** for the missing group against 16.9h observed. They are *shorter*, not longer — this is the check that would have made exclusion dishonest had it failed.
3. **It barely moves.** Pooling all 204 in gives 12.9h against 13.4h, and downwards.

## Measured effect

Built both ways over the same rows:

| | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `median_completion_h` | 7.1 → 7.1 | 12.6 → 12.6 | 15.8 → 15.8 | 12.5 → **12.7** | 15.2 → 15.2 |
| `imputed_n` | 23 | 39 | 53 | 58 | 43 |
| person-hours | +2.9% | +3.4% | +2.4% | +2.3% | +4.4% |
| national availability | −0.0118pp | −0.0154pp | −0.0130pp | −0.0144pp | −0.0200pp |

Four county-months move down one grade: Limerick 04 B→C, Mayo 05 C→D, Monaghan 08 C→D, Offaly 08 B→C. Worst single county-month is Offaly 08 at −0.179pp.

**July's headline moved, and that is a real leak.** `has_end` and `imputed` are OR'd across an event's pins, and a span is the union of its pins' intervals. 5 events of 4,473 carry an observed completion on one pin and an estimate on another, so their spans grew. Same mechanism by which a scheduled-end pin already contributes its announced interval to an event counted as observed — consistent with existing behaviour, but the headline is not perfectly insulated. Separating the tiers needs a second per-tier interval accumulator in `Region` for 0.2h in one month of five.

## Prior art

Ofwat's supply interruptions commitment is the regulated analogue — property-minutes as a total, same shape as availability. Default position is that "there are no exclusions"; where telemetry is unavailable, times come from customer contact, flow/pressure indications or **"verified modelled data"**. It also requires reporting "what proportion of its start/stop times has been informed by each data source", which is what `imputed_n` on the page is.

We **deviate** from its precautionary principle — it resolves uncertainty toward the *highest* interruption value, a category median is central — and that is recorded as a known limitation.

## Also here

- **`cases.first_start_date` (schema v3)** stamps the publication time seen on the first download and never advances it. `COALESCE`, not `MIN`: a backward re-stamp is as real as a forward one, and the minimum rule was measured and rejected. An instrument only — nothing computes a duration from it yet.
- **The `cases` schema is now declared once**, as the ordered `CASE_COLUMNS` mapping. Adding `first_start_date` broke ten tests because the table was written out in three places by hand and two were missed. `TestSchemaIsDeclaredOnce` now fails if a fourth statement reappears; the generated schema SQL is byte-identical to before.
- **`CLAUDE.md`**, a settled-decisions index. `notes/` is ~33k tokens across 10 files and nothing in the repo loaded automatically — which is how this change's first draft came to re-propose a rule that had been measured and rejected four weeks earlier.

## PR #41 — Three cold reviews, triaged
*merged 2026-08-18 · +1040/−158*

### PR body

Three external reviews of this repo — a code review, a usability review, and a methodology review — triaged against `notes/` and applied where they held up. Every claim was re-measured before it was trusted, and a few didn't survive that.

**367 tests pass** (341 before), ruff clean, site builds.

## What changed

**Two latent defects.** `boil_notice_fate`'s accrue branch returned an end before the start for an advance-dated notice, which county arithmetic silently drops but the area history would print as `-240h so far`. And the static county pages printed raw availability while the app clamps at 99.99% — so `c/<county>.html` could claim `100.000%` beside a real outage. Neither was producing a wrong published number; both are shapes the feed can present at any time.

**Contrast.** Three of five grade chips failed WCAG for their white lettering (A 3.35:1, B 2.87:1, D 2.64:1), and `.badge.partial` was **1.79:1** — yellow on off-white. Fills keep their hues; text now draws on separate `*-text` tokens. Every ratio was recomputed independently before the patch was applied.

**Month tabs wrap.** The review said the strip already overflows a 390px phone at five tabs. It doesn't — the buttons shrink to fit. It starts at **six** tabs, overflowing by 42px, which arrives on **1 September** when September's tab appears. Shipped ahead of the fault.

**The health flags are not signals.** Traced from a reader-level observation: Donegal carried a health marker on May. The notice behind it is titled *"Low Pressure - Donegal"* and its description never mentions drinking water. Measured across the corpus — `boil_water_notice` is 100% reliable and 100% redundant with the category; `do_not_drink` is wrong on 9 of 19. Because `classify` tests quality ahead of the hard categories, five burst mains and mains repairs were classed quality and accruing **nothing**. Both flags dropped; category only.

    8 county-months change · 0 change grade
    national person-hours 79,745,765 → 79,819,755 (+0.09%)
    9 false health markers gone

**Do-not-consume notices get lift pairing — but not the staleness exclusion.** The methodology review proposed giving them the whole boil-notice policy. Half was taken. The exclusion rests on case 221165, which sat `Open` while its own text said the notice *"is now lifted with immediate effect"*. No do-not-consume notice on file does that: Whiddy Island (`Open` since 2022) names a real, unlifted water-quality failure. Dropping a stale boil notice that was in fact lifted costs nothing; dropping a live do-not-consume removes a drinking-water warning. `notes/` records what would reopen it.

**A number where there wasn't one.** `uisce-eval-overlap` measures the overlap double-count — the last unquantified limitation in `statuspage-methodology.md` — at **3.6%** of national outage person-hours. Left uncorrected deliberately, with the reasons written down.

**Three doc drifts**, one of which this branch introduced itself: `site.py`'s module docstring still described the removed grade knock; README led with a stale 12% `closed_at` figure (now 1.9% → ~1.1%); and both notes pointed `OBSERVED_END_SOURCES` at `site.py` after it moved to `config.py`.

**Copy.** The health mark is month-scoped like the grade beside it, but said "active", so a May notice read as a live warning. Now tensed to the month on screen, and the tooltip drops "the water may not be safe to drink" on historical months. The in-progress month's caveat says what it means instead of naming the inference pipeline.

- **Text-gating the health flags** instead of dropping them — byte-identical on every case on file, so it buys nothing and adds a prose regex that can rot.
- **Darkening all five grade fills** to keep white text throughout — turns D close enough to F to blur the one boundary the scale exists to draw.
- **The staleness exclusion for do-not-consume notices** — above.
- **A third caveat line naming the inference backlog** — replaced by rewording the caveat already on the page, which stays true whether or not inference is current.

Each is in `notes/` with its numbers, and the three that close something off have rows in `CLAUDE.md`.

  off the same intervals. A do-not-consume notice in force 1 May - 5 July,
  proven by its own lift, showed the drinking-water marker on May and then
  nothing for June — the cap is a person-hours instrument, and grade() already
  records that a health notice's importance is not measured in person-hours.
  
  So the two questions are now separate functions. paired_end says when the
  notice stood until, uncapped, because the lift is evidence about the world.
  charged_end says how much of that it may charge. Case.in_force carries the
  first into Region.knock_iv, which health_n is counted over; boil_notice_fate
  reports the real end again and its caller applies the cap, which is the better
  split anyway — fate answers what happened, resolve_case answers what it costs.
  
  The unpaired path keeps its cap deliberately: there it is a staleness hedge on
  a status field known to go stale, not an accrual ceiling, and lifting it would
  mark every month on the site for Whiddy Island's 1,460 open days on the
  strength of that field alone.
  
  knock_refs went write-only once knock_iv carried the same keys, and is gone.
  No published number moves — the built payload is byte-identical at a fixed now.
